Sunaina inspection cell

A conveyor brings each product to one spot. A servo arm swings the ESP32-CAM around it to take photos from above and from the front, and the ESP32-S3 decides PASS or FAIL.

How it works

The servo stands beside the belt with its shaft lined up with the centre of the product. The camera sits at the end of the arm and always points back at the shaft. When the arm swings, the camera circles the product and stays aimed at it, so one servo gives the top view and the front view. It can also take a back view.

Measured on the rig: front 0°, top 63°, back 120°, arm speed 120°/s. The drawing shows the idea; these are the real servo angles.

One product, start to finish

  1. The product reaches the inspection spot. A sensor sees it and the belt stops.
  2. The arm is up (63°). The camera takes the top photo.
  3. The arm swings to 0°. The camera is now level with the product and takes the front photo.
  4. The arm swings back up, which clears the belt.
  5. The S3 checks both photos and shows PASS or FAIL on the OLED and the output pins.
  6. The belt starts again and the next product comes in.

Open questions

  • Does the belt stop for each product, or keep moving while photos are taken?
  • How does the S3 know a product has arrived: IR sensor, the camera itself, or a timer?
  • Who controls the conveyor motor: the S3, or does it run on its own?
  • Which views matter: top only, top + front, or all three?
  • What size are the products? Arm length depends on this.
  • Lighting: fixed LEDs on the cell, or the ESP32-CAM flash?
  • What happens to a FAIL: just a signal, or a pusher that knocks it off the belt?

Rough sizing: the ESP32-CAM sees about 60° across. With a 15 cm arm, a photo covers about 15 cm at the product, so products up to about 10 cm fit with some margin.
